Why Environment Matters

September 18, 2026

I want a work environment that makes people want to do good work on their own initiative.

When I joined my third startup team, one subject I wanted to share was how to create a motivating environment before we knew the outcome. That mattered to me. A startup already has plenty of things we cannot control. I want the work we do together to be worth our attention.

When I reflected on my work in the past, I often asked whether I was working hard enough or spending enough time. Those are worthwhile questions. I do have things to improve: think proposals through more fully, check work carefully before handing it over, and give other people enough context.

But I also began to ask another question: what kind of environment makes me want to keep doing those things?

Interest is part of it. Whether a problem makes me curious affects whether I want to keep learning about it after the assigned work ends. A challenge alone can hold my attention for a while. Sustained effort also needs me to care about the problem. Recognising that helps me choose my work more carefully.

Feedback matters too. When effort produces no response for a long time, it is difficult to know whether to continue, adjust, or stop. Keeping a house full of snacks and sugary drinks while expecting willpower alone to carry a fitness goal already makes the job harder. A work environment can similarly make some behaviours easier and others more costly.

Across my startup experiences, I noticed something: when there is no positive feedback on direction, an organisation can turn to assigning more tasks and seeking certainty in execution. Everyone has work to do. Being busy still leaves a question unanswered: is this work taking us closer to the result we want?

I am part of that system too. As a product lead, I can also use another plan or another requirement to make myself feel temporarily more certain. So I want to begin with my own responsibility: explain the goal and the reasons. Help the people working with me understand what we are trying to solve, why it matters, and what we still do not know.

I also want to bring feedback closer to the work. Show what we have made early enough for someone to respond. Raise questions before they turn into rework. When someone disagrees with a proposal, discuss their reasons. When an attempt does not produce the outcome we wanted, explain what we learned before deciding what comes next.

None of this guarantees a successful business. I hope it gives people more chances to exercise judgment and see how their contribution affects the product, rather than simply receiving the next task.

Reading once gave me the courage to leave work environments I did not like. Now I also want to practise creating an environment I would choose to stay in. Choosing a team and deciding how I work with others every day are both responsibilities I can take.

A good environment needs people to make it, day after day. I want to be one of them.
