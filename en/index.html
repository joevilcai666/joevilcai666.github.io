About

These past few years I've been building products at AI startups. Once I started working, I noticed something: the things that don't deserve anyone's attention — yet still have to be dealt with — are exactly the ones that keep taking up our time. So I'm building Origin, a product that arranges your exercise and meals, tracks your daily spending, and handles your work tasks.

While polishing Origin, I meant to focus on the product itself, but kept getting pulled into back-and-forths with coding agents, debugging. That experience gave me the urge to build a second product, Craft — one that lets people spend their attention on what to do, why to do it, and whether what gets built is actually what they want.

In my past startups, when positive feedback was missing, the company would respond by assigning people more tasks. It made me realize: when an organization can't reduce uncertainty about direction, it tends to increase certainty about execution instead. These experiences left me with a question I keep coming back to: what kind of environment makes people willing to commit, brave enough to judge, and able to create?

I love reading. Other people's words gave me the courage to leave work environments I didn't like and go look for what I truly wanted to do. They also made me see that money lets me say “no” to what I dislike. Life is full of questions that puzzle and intrigue me, so I write to think the vague ones through — and maybe my perspective can offer you, the reader, one more reference.
